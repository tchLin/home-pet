NFC feeding stick

Place an NTAG213 sticker near the tip of a plastic stick; place PN532 antenna behind a thin nonmetallic console surface away from the LCD metal backing and battery. Use USB serial from the PC to the ESP32 to register a tag UID or write a short identifier through the PN532. Map IDs to actions in firmware; require tag removal and a cooldown between feeds. [S7, S9]

Voice without speaking back

Start with push-to-listen and a few offline commands (jump, sleep, wake up). ESP-IDF + ESP-SR MultiNet is the software path; configure I²S capture and convert microphone samples to the expected 16 kHz mono format. Wake-word mode is a later optimization; no free-form conversation or audio output in scope. [S6, S8, S12]

Display and optics

The Waveshare reference module measures 58 × 35 mm, with 40.8 × 30.6 mm active area. A 30 mm cube covers only part of it. Start with a centered 200 × 200 px viewport (25.5 × 25.5 mm), black surroundings and a mirror/orientation correction. Verify brightness, color fringing and clipping before committing to an enclosure. [S2]

Interfaces & electrical limits

Use 3.3 V logic on ESP32 GPIO. LCD and PN532 can share SPI clock/data lines with separate chip selects if compatible; use a separate SPI bus if needed. I²S is independent. All grounds common. Confirm the exact PN532 module voltage and pull-ups; verify regulator current capacity before powering peripherals. [S1, S2, S8, S9]

Concept only. Use secure, insulated power connections and verify the exact DevKit power input. Switch disables output, not necessarily battery charging. A switch connection to EN may require soldering.

Important: #6106 has a documented startup limitation around 200 mA of immediate load, despite its 1 A maximum running output. Test with the bare ESP32 first, then add peripherals individually. Software-delayed initialization alone may not eliminate physical inrush; hardware load sequencing or a different supply may be necessary. [S3, S4]

Battery safety and charging

  • Use #2011 with #6106; set the charge jumper to 500 mA as recommended by the battery vendor, with all power disconnected before modifying the board.
  • Verify battery connector polarity and 5 V output with a multimeter. Never connect a raw cell to the ESP32 3.3 V or 5 V pin.
  • Use either PC USB or external power on the DevKit, not both, unless the exact board explicitly permits it. Disconnect the boost output before PC programming.
  • Do not short, bend, puncture or charge/use the cell unattended. Keep it isolated from the glass, sharp pins, loose wiring and heat. Do not use a damaged or swollen cell.
  • The battery has no built-in thermistor; charge in a suitable environment. The board also has a six-hour charge safety timeout. [S1, S4, S5]

Runtime planning

3.6–6.3 hours

Illustrative continuous runtime at 200–350 mA average 5 V load, using 2,000 mAh × 3.7 V × 85% efficiency. Actual runtime can be lower.


5.0 h

Measure actual steady and peak current, battery discharge rating and regulator temperature. LCD backlight, NFC polling, and always-on audio influence runtime. A 5 V USB power bank is the simplest fallback if the integrated charger is unstable.

Implementation

Recommended assembly order

USB power and ESP32 bring-up

Flash a basic program; verify PSRAM detection and identify exact board pinout. Keep battery disconnected.

LCD + prism

Display a test pattern and animate one sprite. Check reflection orientation, visible color, brightness and optical clipping.

NFC accessories

Test one card and one small sticker, register IDs over serial, and verify repeated tap/removal behavior.

Voice commands

Test microphone capture and push-to-listen recognition; record false positives and recognition accuracy for a few phrases.

Integrated USB operation

Run graphics, NFC, BLE and recognition together; measure current, responsiveness, memory and temperature.

Battery test and enclosure

Configure charger, measure voltage and startup current, test cold starts, charging and sustained load; only then plan the portable housing.
